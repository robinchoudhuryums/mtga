#: name: Grand Larceny — Outlaw Heist
#: format: standard
#: colors: BR
#: plan: midrange
#: archetype: Rakdos (B/R) variant of 44 — the same mono-black heist spine, taking the
#: archetype: RED side of the fork instead of the blue one. Where 44 spends its splash
#: archetype: on two one-shot ETB thefts (Azula, Etrata), this deck spends it on
#: archetype: REPEATABLE ENGINES: Laughing Jasper Flint steals off the top of an
#: archetype: opponent's library EVERY UPKEEP, scaling with the number of outlaws you
#: archetype: control, and Rakdos, the Muscle steals every time you sacrifice a creature.
#: archetype: THE OUTLAW COUNT IS THE POINT. Jasper scales on Assassins, Mercenaries,
#: archetype: Pirates, Rogues and Warlocks, and eleven of this deck's creatures are
#: archetype: outlaws: both Tinybones and Black Cat, Cunning Thief (Rogue), Reno and Rude,
#: archetype: Raven Eagle and Sawblade Skinripper (Assassin), Ghost, Spectral Saboteur
#: archetype: (Rogue), Hama (Warlock), Laughing Jasper Flint himself (Rogue), Rakdos, the
#: archetype: Muscle and The Infamous Cruelclaw (Mercenary). Better still, Jasper's first
#: archetype: line — "creatures you control but don't own are Mercenaries" — means every
#: archetype: creature this deck STEALS becomes an outlaw and grows the next trigger. The
#: archetype: engine feeds itself, which is the thing 44 cannot do.
#: archetype: Ghost, Spectral Saboteur earns a slot here that it did not earn in 44: it
#: archetype: heists nothing, but an unblockable body is an outlaw that always connects,
#: archetype: and outlaw COUNT is a resource in this list.
#: archetype: RAKDOS NEEDS OUTLETS, which the first draft did not give him. His trigger is
#: archetype: "whenever you SACRIFICE another creature", and the only non-combat outlet in
#: archetype: the deck was his own once-per-turn ability — a protected build-around
#: archetype: enabled by itself. Umbral Collar Zealot (free, repeatable) and Sawblade
#: archetype: Skinripper ({2}, and itself an Assassin with menace that PAYS OFF the
#: archetype: sacrifices with damage) fix that; `deck.py engines` now reads four sacrifice
#: archetype: enablers rather than two.
#: archetype: NO GRAVEYARD HATE. Strategic Betrayal and Pit of Offerings were both cut for
#: archetype: the same reason, which is easy to miss because both read as fine cards on
#: archetype: their own: they EXILE an opponent's graveyard, and four of this deck's heist
#: archetype: cards — Tinybones the Pickpocket, Shark Shredder, Hama and Rakdos — need that
#: archetype: graveyard FULL. The discard package fills it; nothing here should empty it.
#: archetype: RED IS A REAL SECOND COLOR, NOT A SPLASH — Jasper, Rakdos, Cruelclaw and
#: archetype: Korvold each want a hard {R}, so the manabase runs ten owned B/R duals
#: archetype: rather than 44's ten blue sources for two cards. Every red card is a SINGLE
#: archetype: {R} pip by design: Decadent Dragon was drafted here for its {2}{B} adventure
#: archetype: half and cut again once `consistency` priced its {2}{R}{R} front face at
#: archetype: 53% on turn four — the deck cannot support a double-red card, and grading
#: archetype: the card by the half you want rather than the face you cast is exactly the
#: archetype: rationalization the front-face costing rule exists to prevent.
#: archetype: DISTINCTNESS vs 1 Black Sun, the roster's other Rakdos deck: Black Sun is
#: archetype: aggro-sacrifice with a 5/7 clock and card advantage 0 — it wins by racing.
#: archetype: This is a midrange attrition deck that wins by spending the opponent's
#: archetype: cards. They share the sacrifice tag and nothing else structural.
#: tier: B — PROVISIONAL, unplayed and craft-heavy like its core deck. Graded from the
#: tier: same rubric as 44 and held at the same letter for the same reasons: the engine
#: tier: is combat- and sacrifice-triggered so it must connect, the heist package reads
#: tier: as unclassified to the role model so the measurable card advantage understates
#: tier: it, and every effect is a singleton. The variant's specific argument for being
#: tier: worth its own list rather than a merge into 44 is that its two red cards are
#: tier: REPEATABLE where 44's two blue cards are one-shot, which is a different deck
#: tier: rather than a different five cards. Re-grade once both have been played.
#: protect: Laughing Jasper Flint; Rakdos, the Muscle; Tinybones, Bauble Burglar; Tinybones, the Pickpocket; Black Widow, Super Spy; Cruelclaw's Heist

#~ -Ghost, Spectral Saboteur | +Etali, Primal Storm | CRAFT (R): Etali attacks and casts
#~ the top of EVERY opponent's library free — the biggest repeatable heist in red, and a
#~ real threat rather than an outlaw-counter body. Ghost's only job here is being an
#~ unblockable outlaw, which is the softest slot in the deck.
#~ -Raven Eagle | +Vaan, Street Thief | CRAFT (R): Raven Eagle's "whenever you draw your
#~ second card each turn" clause is near-dead here (Phyrexian Arena is the only repeatable
#~ draw), so it does half a card's work. Vaan is a red heist body on the same axis.
#~ -Corrupt Court Official | +Fireglass Mentor | CRAFT (U): a B/R two-drop that impulses
#~ every turn an opponent lost life — note it exiles from YOUR library, so it is card
#~ advantage, NOT a heist, and it does not feed Tinybones, Bauble Burglar.
#~ note: the open question is the SACRIFICE density. Rakdos, the Muscle and Sothera both
#~ want creatures dying, and the deck now fields four enablers but few expendable bodies —
#~ every creature here is doing a job. A token-maker would serve both better than a fourth
#~ outlet; none of the on-color ones surveyed were worth a maindeck slot yet.
#~ -Ozai's Cruelty | +Superior Foes of Spider-Man | CRAFT (U): the best of the SELF-impulse
#~ cards for this deck specifically, and not for its impulse — it is a Human Rogue Villain,
#~ i.e. an OUTLAW, so it feeds Laughing Jasper Flint's count on a 3-mana trample body. Its
#~ own trigger ("cast a spell with mana value 4 or greater") fires on only 8 of 36 nonlands
#~ here, so grade it as an outlaw that sometimes impulses, not as a card-advantage engine.
#~ note: SELF-EXILE vs HEIST — they do NOT combine, they COMPETE, and the pool says so.
#~ Exactly ONE Standard card treats "a card in exile you may cast" as one resource
#~ regardless of whose it is: Interdimensional Web Watch, whose restricted mana is now
#~ maindecked in both 44 and 44a. The real "cast from exile matters" PAYOFFS exist but are
#~ off-color for a black deck — Quintorius Kand (W/R, owned: 2 damage whenever you cast a
#~ spell from exile) and Doc Aurlock, Grizzled Genius (U/G: those spells cost {2} less).
#~ That is a Boros or Simic deck, not this one. Within these colors, heist beats self-
#~ impulse for the same slot on three counts: nearly every heist card says "mana of any
#~ type", so a stolen spell costs no color commitment; it is disruption as well as a card;
#~ and it spends THEIR card quality instead of thinning your own library.
