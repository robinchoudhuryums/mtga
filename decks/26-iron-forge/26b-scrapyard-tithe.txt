#: name: Iron Forge — Scrapyard Tithe
#: format: Standard
#: colors: UR
#: based-on: deck.txt
#: plan: midrange
#: protect: Monument to Endurance; Cool but Rude; Magmakin Artillerist; Summon: Bahamut; Death to Our Enemies
#: archetype: IZZET ARTIFACTS THAT WIN WITHOUT ATTACKING. Every other deck in this family gets
#: archetype: its damage through the combat step — 26 with ramped bombs, 26a with poison, 47
#: archetype: with affinity bodies, 48 with anthems, 48a with crewed Vehicles. This one taxes
#: archetype: the opponent every time you throw something away. Treasure and cheap artifacts are
#: archetype: the currency; discarding and sacrificing them is how you spend it.
#: archetype: THE THREE DRAIN ENGINES all read off the same action. Monument to Endurance:
#: archetype: "whenever you DISCARD a card, choose one — draw a card; create a Treasure; EACH
#: archetype: OPPONENT LOSES 3 LIFE." Cool but Rude at level 2: "whenever you discard a card,
#: archetype: this Class deals 2 damage to each opponent" — and its level 1 gives you a free
#: archetype: discard-and-draw on every attack, so it fuels itself. Magmakin Artillerist:
#: archetype: "whenever you discard one or more cards, this creature deals that much damage to
#: archetype: each opponent." With two of the three out, one loot is 5 damage across the table.
#: archetype: THE DISCARD IS NEVER A COST HERE, which is what makes the engine work rather than
#: archetype: just hurt. Collector's Vault loots AND makes a Treasure; Melded Moxite discards to
#: archetype: draw TWO; Seize the Spoils discards to draw two and make a Treasure; Cool but Rude
#: archetype: replaces the card it discards; Inti exiles the top card and lets you play it. Every
#: archetype: one of those is card-neutral or better BEFORE the drain triggers.
#: archetype: DESTROY-TO-DAMAGE is the second half. Scrap Compactor, Barrels of Blasting Jelly
#: archetype: and Bumbleflower's Sharepot each sacrifice themselves to kill something; Hell to
#: archetype: Pay converts overkill damage back into Treasure; Crime Novelist turns every
#: archetype: artifact sacrifice into a +1/+1 counter and a red mana. Armor Wars III and
#: archetype: Weftstalker Ardent add non-combat damage that does not care about blockers either.
#: archetype: THE LOOT ENGINES ARE THE ENGINE, not filler: Kitsa and Gran-Gran each loot for
#: archetype: free every turn, Rewrite History loots whenever your creatures become TAPPED
#: archetype: (which this deck does constantly for mana), and Kiora draws two and discards two
#: archetype: on entry — two drain triggers off one card. Galvanize is priced by the same plan:
#: archetype: 3 damage, or FIVE if you have drawn two or more cards this turn, which a looting
#: archetype: deck manages most turns.
#: archetype: DISTINCTNESS from its own parent: deck 26 and this deck share colours, an artifact
#: archetype: base and a taste for big top-end, but 26 needs its permanents to CONNECT and this
#: archetype: one is indifferent to whether it has a creature at all.
#: notes: BUILT 2026-07 from the same 122-card pile as 48 and 48a.
#: notes: UNGRADED ON PURPOSE — no `#: tier:` until played.
#: notes: WHY THIS IS NOT THE DECK ORIGINALLY PROPOSED. The plan floated for a 26 variant was
#: notes: "Treasure ramp into unique heavy-hitters". That is exactly what 48a Motor Pool already
#: notes: does — Treasure engine, Brass's Bounty, Radiant Lotus, Summon: Bahamut — so building
#: notes: it here would have produced a near-duplicate of a deck finished the same day. The
#: notes: DESTROY-TO-DAMAGE half of that brief had no home anywhere on the roster, so the
#: notes: variant was built on that axis instead. Treasure is still the fuel; the difference is
#: notes: what it buys.
#: notes: THE FIRST DRAFT DUPLICATED 48a AND WAS PIVOTED RATHER THAN TUNED. Built as a
#: notes: Treasure-ramp-to-heavy-hitters deck it measured `similar` 84% against 48a with
#: notes: FOURTEEN shared nonland cards — the same meaningful-overlap pattern 26a vs 26 showed,
#: notes: not a tag artifact. The shared half was the top end: Summon: Bahamut, Radiant Lotus,
#: notes: Brass's Bounty, Extinguisher Battleship and The Eternity Elevator all went to 48a
#: notes: exclusively, and this deck took five cheap repeatable LOOT engines instead. Result:
#: notes: avg MV 2.97 -> 2.31, early drops 18 -> 21, card advantage 5 -> 7, and overlap down to
#: notes: 81% / 11 shared, of which five are the commodity Izzet removal suite every deck in
#: notes: the family runs. The two decks now want opposite curves.
#: notes: TRUCK TOSS WAS CUT FOR THE SAME REASON IN MINIATURE: it costs {2} less if you control
#: notes: a Vehicle, and this deck runs zero — it was a 4-mana Shock here and a 2-mana one in
#: notes: 48a. Galvanize replaced it and is priced by THIS deck's plan instead.
#: notes: AGNA QEL'A IS A LOOT ENGINE IN THE MANABASE ("{2}{U}, {T}: draw a card, then discard
#: notes: a card"), so the drain has a source that costs no spell slot and survives a sweeper.
#: notes: NO SWEEPER AND ALMOST NO BLOCKERS is the obvious hole — the deck's creature count is
#: notes: deliberately low because creatures are not how it wins. Against a fast aggro start it
#: notes: must trade removal one-for-one and race with drain, which is a real losing posture.
#: notes: The five interaction spells are doing double duty as both defence and reach.
