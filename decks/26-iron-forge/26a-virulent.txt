#~ note: CORRECTED 2026-07 — an earlier version of this note claimed "ZERO Robot-count payoffs in all of Standard, any colour" and used that to decline a Robot-tribal variant. THAT WAS WRONG, and the error was methodological: the search regex looked for the literal word "Robot" ("Robots you control get", "for each Robot", ...), and the entire CHOOSE-A-CREATURE-TYPE category never contains the type name. There are 14 such cards in U/R/colorless Standard and FIVE are genuine lords that work by naming Robot: Lifecraft Engine (OWNED — +1/+1 to the chosen type, and Vehicles you control BECOME that type), Chronicle of Victory (OWNED — +2/+2, first strike, trample, and a draw on every chosen-type spell), Adaptive Automaton, Patchwork Banner and Banner of Kinship. Also in that category: Roaming Throne (DOUBLES triggered abilities of the chosen type), Collective Inferno (doubles damage from chosen-type sources), and Leyline of Transformation (OWNED — makes ALL your creatures the chosen type, i.e. it turns every lord on for the whole board). Robot tribal is viable; the original sweep simply could not see the payoffs. Lesson, and it is the same one this repo keeps relearning: do not gate a search on a literal name when the effect can be expressed generically.
#~ note: TOOL CAVEAT FOUND WHILE CHECKING THAT. `deck.py tribes` reports "Type-matters payoffs — rewards Robot" and named six cards for deck 26 (Ultron, Ultron Drone, Castle Doom, Mouser Foundry, Kavaron Memorial World, Donatello Mutant Mechanic). Every one of those CREATES a Robot token; none of them rewards the Robot COUNT. The heuristic reads token-creation as type-matters support, which overstates tribal depth. Earlier reasoning in this deck's build cited those six as Robot payoffs — the honest number is ONE (Chronicle of Victory). Read that section of `tribes` as "cards that mention this type", not "cards that reward it".
#~ note: THE MANA STRUCTURE FINDING, which is the useful part of the Robot sweep. Of 27 owned Robots: 9 are COLORLESS, 11 mono-BLUE, 5 mono-RED, 2 hybrid/gold. The Robot pool is blue-and-colorless; red barely contributes bodies. Measured on the live lists: deck 26 runs 11 red-requiring nonland cards of which only THREE are creatures, and 26a runs 8 of which only TWO are creatures. In both decks red is buying REMOVAL (Abrade, Plasma Bolt, Fiery Annihilation, Explosive Derailment, Full Bore, Price of Freedom, Untimely Malfunction), not the creature base. 26a is the deck where that is worth re-examining: it is the aggro list, tapped lands hurt it most, and 18 of its nonland cards already want blue against 8 wanting red. A near-mono-blue 26a would have essentially no mana problems, at the cost of trading burn for bounce and counters. NOT changed here — flagged as the next real architectural question for this deck.
#~ note: A THIRD "ARTIFACT TOKENS" VARIANT WAS INVESTIGATED AND DECLINED — recorded here so the pool sweep is not re-run. The ENABLERS are abundant: 120 artifact/token makers in U/R/colorless Standard, 40 of them owned. The PAYOFF is what does not exist. Sacrifice-an-artifact drain (the Marionette Master axis that makes a token deck actually kill) is ONE card, Vengeful Tracker, and it triggers when the OPPONENT sacrifices — so that axis is effectively zero. Token-COUNT payoffs: three cards, one owned (Mister Fantastic, which draws rather than wins). Mass token animation: one card, The Mechanist, and it animates one token per turn. Static artifact-creature anthems: Chrome Dome (+1/+0, Rare craft) and Krang. Everything else that "cares" is an artifact-COUNT payoff — affinity, improvise, "+1/+0 for each artifact" — and a Treasure feeds those exactly as well as Krang does, except Krang also wins the game. A token variant would therefore be core 26 with strictly worse artifacts and no unique win condition.

#~ note: Improvised Arsenal belongs in CORE 26, not here — "+1/+0 for each artifact you control" is pure damage, and poison does not scale with power. Tezzeret, Cruel Captain is not worth a Mythic for either deck: its -3 only tutors artifacts with mana value 1 or less, which cannot find Virulent Silencer (MV 3) or any of core 26's heavy hitters.
#~ +Trade the Helm | CRAFT (Uncommon) — {4}{U} "exchange control of target artifact or creature you control and target artifact or creature an opponent controls", cycling {2}. A genuinely good idea for this deck: hand them a Treasure or a 1/1 and take the blocker that is stopping the clock, and it is never dead thanks to cycling. Parked purely on cost — five mana is a lot in a deck with avg MV 2.97 and sixteen early drops. Take it if the deck keeps losing to a single large blocker.
#~ +Enter the Enigma | OWNED — {U} sorcery: target creature can't be blocked, draw a card. A second Pym Particles minus the vigilance. Lost its slot to Mechan Navigator because card advantage had fallen to 1 and the Navigator both draws AND is a carrier; take Enter the Enigma if you would rather have a second guaranteed connection than a body.
#~ +Cryptic Coat | CRAFT (Rare) — {2}{U} Equipment: "equipped creature gets +1/+0 and can't be blocked", plus it cloaks the top card of your library and attaches itself. PERMANENT unblockability is the strongest version of this deck's key effect. Note the cloaked 2/2 is NOT an artifact creature, so move the Coat onto a real carrier. Parked as the one Rare worth considering.
#~ +Ragged Playmate | CRAFT (Common) — {1}{R} artifact creature (so a carrier itself) with "{1}, {T}: target creature with power 2 or less can't be blocked". Most of this deck's carriers are power 2 or less, so it is repeatable unblockability on a body. Cheapest way to add a second repeatable enabler after Atomic Microsizer.
#~ +Deserter's Disciple | OWNED — {1}{R}, "{T}: another target creature you control with power 2 or less can't be blocked". Same effect as Ragged Playmate for zero wildcards, but it is NOT an artifact creature, so it adds no carrier and no artifact count.
#~ +Fin Fang Foom | OWNED — has EIGHT artifact-targeting spells to copy in this deck against four in core 26, so its engine is twice as live here. Excluded anyway because it is not an artifact creature and cannot carry poison, and {2}{R}{R} is the worst cost in either deck. A defensible add if you want the spell-copy value.
