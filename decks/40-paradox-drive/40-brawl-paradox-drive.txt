#: name: Paradox Drive — Brawl
#: format: Brawl
#: colors: GU
#: based-on: deck.txt
#: commander: Ignis Scientia
#: archetype: Brawl conversion of the Simic (G/U) big-mana ramp-CONTROL deck (deck 40).
#: archetype: The Standard list was already 60-card singleton with no off-identity cards,
#: archetype: so this is the same 60 with Ignis Scientia leading from the command zone.
#: archetype: The plan is unchanged: ramp behind a counter/removal shell into a
#: archetype: game-ending X-spell (Finale of Revelation / Mind Spring), with Taigam
#: archetype: copying the payoff and Paradox Surveyor smoothing the draws.
#: notes: WHY IGNIS SCIENTIA, AND WHY NOT THE TWO OBVIOUS PICKS. Only THREE of this
#: notes: deck's legends carry the required G/U identity — Ignis Scientia, The Goose
#: notes: Mother, Absorbing Man. The other five (Taigam, Y'shtola, Wan Shi Tong, Namor,
#: notes: Kiora) are MONO-U, and a mono-U commander makes every green card in the deck
#: notes: illegal, so they were never candidates however good they are inside the 60.
#: notes: THE GOOSE MOTHER WAS THE FIRST PICK AND WAS REJECTED ON ROTATION. She is the
#: notes: best mechanical fit by some distance — an {X}{G}{U} spell, so the commander is
#: notes: itself a mana sink that scales with the ramp, entering with X counters, making
#: notes: half-X Food and turning each Food into a card on attack; commander tax is
#: notes: unusually cheap on her since recasting just means paying less X. But she is WOE
#: notes: and `deck.py rotation` puts her at ~2026, i.e. within months. A rotating card in
#: notes: the 60 is a swap; a rotating COMMANDER is the deck's identity expiring, so the
#: notes: mechanical fit does not survive the calendar. She stays in the 60, where she is
#: notes: still an excellent X-payoff until she rotates.
#: notes: Ignis Scientia (FIN, safe to ~2028) is the rotation-safe pick that is still ON
#: notes: PLAN: his ETB looks at six and puts a LAND onto the battlefield, which is
#: notes: exactly what a deck that wins off a big X-spell wants from a card it can cast
#: notes: every game — and the graveyard-exile activation is live in a singleton format
#: notes: full of recursion. Absorbing Man (MSH, safe to ~2029) was the runner-up and is
#: notes: the better raw card, but his copy clause needs a strong artifact / non-Aura
#: notes: enchantment / land to point at and this deck runs almost none, so he would
#: notes: usually be a 4/4 vigilance copying a land. Both stay in the 60; switching is a
#: notes: one-line `#: commander:` change with no card swaps.
#: notes: WHAT THIS CONVERSION ALREADY HAS that deck 3's Brawl notes flag as the usual
#: notes: gap: 26 lands and card advantage 6. A 60-card Standard deck moved to Brawl
#: notes: normally wants more of both, because singleton 1v1 games run long and you
#: notes: cannot lean on redundancy — this deck was built that way already, which is why
#: notes: it was picked first of the four planned conversions.
#: notes: TUNING FROM HERE, not yet applied: protection is 0 in the Standard list and
#: notes: that matters MORE in Brawl, where the opponent knows your commander from turn
#: notes: zero and can hold removal for her. The counterspell suite is the current
#: notes: answer; a hexproof/ward effect is the first real add. Also watch that the
#: notes: singleton X-spell payoffs (Finale, Mind Spring, Portent, Divergent Equation)
#: notes: are 4 shots, and 2 of them — Mind Spring and Portent (BLB) — rotate ~2027, so
#: notes: the win condition itself thins out a year after the commander question settles.
#: notes: Faithful game-type conversion, not a from-scratch Brawl build — a starting
#: notes: point to tune once it has been played.
