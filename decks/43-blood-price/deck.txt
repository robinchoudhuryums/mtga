#: archetype: Orzhov (W/B) LIFE-AS-CURRENCY attrition. The premise: cards whose strong

#: archetype: — exchanging totals refunds every point you spent and drops them to yours.
#: archetype: TWO PLANS THAT WIN WITHOUT HIM, by design, because he is a single mythic:
#: archetype: (1) TOKEN DRAIN — Bitterbloom Bearer, Lunar Convocation's Bats and the

#: archetype: build an evasive board while Bloodchief Ascension and Mai turn every
#: archetype: exchange into life loss; (2) pure CARD-ADVANTAGE attrition — ten removal
#: archetype: spells backed by six draw effects simply out-resources a fair deck.
#: archetype: DISTINCTNESS vs 42 Darkforce Inversion, the other Mister Negative deck:
#: archetype: 42 is a BLINK deck that re-triggers his ETB to convert a life gap into
#: archetype: cards while keeping its own total high. This one never blinks, runs almost
#: archetype: no ETB-value creatures, and deliberately drives its OWN total DOWN — the
#: archetype: swap is a one-shot refund and stabiliser, not a repeatable draw engine.
#: archetype: Opposite life postures, opposite creature bases, same finisher.
#: tier: B — Orzhov life-as-currency attrition, one band BELOW the A metrics floor,
#: tier: PROVISIONAL (unplayed brew). The floor reads A on interaction 13 (11 of it

#: tier: card-advantage number is REAL — Cruel Truths, Circle of Power, Decode
#: tier: Transmissions and Visions of Villainy each draw two, so this is an engine, not
#: tier: a pile of cantrips. Held at B by two structural risks the floor cannot see:
#: tier: 1. The deck lowers its OWN life total as a core mechanic while running ZERO
#: tier:    protection, so it is spending the exact resource that keeps it alive. Against
#: tier:    an aggro deck the pay-life draws are a liability, and the Mister Negative
#: tier:    refund is SMALLEST in that matchup — the swap scales with the opponent's
#: tier:    total, so it pays most against the control decks that were never racing you.
#: tier: 2. The clock is slow. Three drain permanents and a few evasive bodies is a real
#: tier:    plan B, but it wins on turn 12, not turn 6, which gives the life payments a
#: tier:    long time to matter.
#: tier: PATH TO A: cheap lifelink or a protection effect for the engine pieces, and a
#: tier: faster drain finish so plan B closes before the life debt does.
#: protect: Mister Negative; Lunar Convocation; Phyrexian Arena
#: notes: HOW THE REFUND WORKS — exchanging totals only draws cards when YOUR life was
#: notes: higher, which is not this deck's situation. Here the swap is the good half:
#: notes: you are low, they are high, so you take their total and drop them to yours.
#: notes: Zero cards, a huge swing, and every point spent on draw comes back at once.
#: notes: RELIABILITY, stated honestly: one copy of a 7-drop is ~22% to be seen by turn
#: notes: 7. That is WHY the two backup plans exist — the pay-life cards are individually
#: notes: worth playing at full price, so a Negative-less game is a normal attrition deck
#: notes: rather than a deck that killed itself. Do not add more life payment than the
#: notes: backup plans can survive on their own.
#: notes: The refund also scales with THEIR total, so it is largest against control and
#: notes: smallest against the aggro decks that punish a low life total — Healer's Hawk,
#: notes: Sephiroth's Intervention and the removal suite are the hedge for that matchup.
#: notes: Craft targets: Mister Negative (M) only — everything else is owned.
